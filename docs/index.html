Break reminders that follow your real activity.

Stillbreak sits in your menu bar and counts the time you actually spend working. Step away and the break counts. Keep going and it tells you once.

A stopwatch can’t tell you went to lunch.

A fixed timer ticks whether you’re typing or making coffee. Stillbreak reads how long it has been since your last keyboard, mouse, scroll or tablet input, and records a gap of five minutes or more as a break. Work and break lengths are yours to set; the defaults are 25 and 5 minutes.

Stillbreak

Counts real work. Time away becomes a break, and the next stretch starts fresh.

Small, quiet, and on your side.

  • Counts real work

    Short pauses stay part of the work. Only a gap that reaches your break length is recorded as a break, so reading a long email or thinking through a problem doesn’t reset the clock.

  • Private by design

    Stillbreak reads one thing from macOS: how long since your last input. It does not record keys, pointer positions, app names or window titles. No Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission, and no network service, telemetry or account. Read the details or read the source.

  • The countdown lives in your menu bar

    Time left is always visible, with no window to open. At zero you get one notification (banner and sound can each be turned off), and the clock keeps running into overtime until you take a break.

  • See your week, keep your data

    The Dashboard lays out 3, 7 or 14 days with work in blue and overtime in red, and exports to CSV or JSON. History is one file on your Mac and stays there until you delete it.

Download and install

Stillbreak isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to confirm the first launch. It takes under a minute.

Download for macOS

Latest release on GitHub. Requires macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Apple Silicon and Intel.

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Stillbreak onto the Applications shortcut.
  2. Open Stillbreak from Applications. Install it there before the first launch; running it from the disk image or Downloads can make launch at login fail.
  3. macOS blocks the first launch. Allow it once, using the steps for your version below.

macOS 15 Sequoia or macOS 26 Tahoe

Right-click > Open no longer works on these versions.

  1. Open Stillbreak once and dismiss the warning (Done or OK). Don’t move it to the Trash.
  2. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security and scroll to Security.
  3. Click Open Anyway next to the message about Stillbreak, then enter your password or use Touch ID.
  4. Click Open when the warning appears again.

Open Anyway only appears after a blocked launch, and only for a limited time. If it’s missing, open Stillbreak again first.

macOS 14 Sonoma

  1. Control-click (or right-click) Stillbreak in Applications and choose Open.
  2. Click Open in the dialog.

The Privacy & Security steps also work on Sonoma.

Prefer Terminal?

This removes the download quarantine flag so macOS stops asking. Only do it for a download you trust.

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Stillbreak.app
Install with one Terminal command

This downloads the latest release, checks its SHA-256 checksum and installs it. Files fetched this way don’t get the quarantine flag, so the first-launch warning normally doesn’t appear.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VladimirLi/Stillbreak/main/install.sh | sh

You can read the script first. Options such as --from-source and --uninstall are described in the README.

Why the warning appears

Stillbreak is free and open source, and releases are only ad-hoc signed, not signed with an Apple Developer ID or notarized. Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership, which the project doesn’t have yet. The warning means Apple hasn’t scanned this build, not that it’s harmful. You can read the source, build it yourself, or compare your download with the SHA-256 checksums in the release notes (shasum -a 256 <file>).

Free, and staying free.

Stillbreak has no paid tier and no locked features. If it earns a place in your menu bar, you can chip in. It’s optional, and nothing changes if you don’t.
